# Genre Guides

## Dubstep

Core feel:
- 140 to 150 BPM, half-time weight.
- Kick often beat 1, snare/clap on beat 3.
- Bass phrases leave room for drum hits.
- Drops rely on contrast: sparse sub impact plus mid-bass answer phrases.

Useful moves:
- Create call/response bass clips with root/fifth/octave jumps.
- Automate Auto Filter, Roar, Saturator, or rack macros per phrase.
- Tighten sub lengths and sidechain to kick.
- Use pre-drop silence, vocal chop, or fakeout to increase impact.

Avoid:
- Constant sub note changes through every kick.
- Wide stereo below low mids.
- Too much reverb on first drop hit.

## Tech House

Core feel:
- 124 to 128 BPM.
- Four-on-the-floor kick, rolling bass, syncopated percussion.
- Tight low end and dry, functional mix.

Useful moves:
- Create offbeat bass stabs and 1/16 ghost percussion.
- Keep bass short and sidechained.
- Use filter automation and delay throws for transitions.
- Organize drums into bus plus short room/verb return.

Avoid:
- Overly cinematic drops when the groove should stay locked.
- Long sub tails that blur the kick.

## Experimental Bass

Core feel:
- Tempo flexible, often half-time or broken.
- Texture, negative space, and unusual rhythm are part of the hook.
- Sound design can be more important than dense arrangement.

Useful moves:
- Build sparse bass punctuation with automated timbre changes.
- Use resampling prep for printing sound-design passes.
- Use odd clip lengths, probability, and velocity deviations for unstable percussion.
- Use call/response between tonal bass and noisy artifacts.

Avoid:
- Filling every gap unless the request asks for maximal chaos.
- Making every layer wide and distorted at once.

## Glitch Hop

Core feel:
- 90 to 110 BPM.
- Funky swing, chopped drums, stutter edits, syncopated bass.
- Bass can be heavy but usually grooves rather than only slams.

Useful moves:
- Humanize hats and ghost snares.
- Duplicate short MIDI regions with transposition and velocity variation.
- Add glitch fills through clip splits, short repeats, and automation.
- Use wah/filter movement on bass phrases.

Avoid:
- Quantizing all timing too rigidly.
- Over-compressing the groove until swing disappears.

## Future Bass

Core feel:
- 140 to 160 BPM or 70 to 80 half-time.
- Big chords, sidechain pump, bright lead layers, emotional progressions.
- Drops often balance wide supersaws with clean sub.

Useful moves:
- Layer wide chord/saw tracks under leads while high-passing low content.
- Automate volume/filter movement in chord clips.
- Sidechain music and bass buses to kick.
- Add risers and reverb throws before drops.

Avoid:
- Letting wide chord lows mask the sub.
- Using the same pump depth on every layer if the groove feels flat.

## DnB

Core feel:
- 170 to 176 BPM.
- Fast break or two-step drums, strong sub movement, bass call/response.
- Arrangement energy depends on drum edits and bass punctuation.

Useful moves:
- Program kick/snare anchors and ghost hats carefully.
- Use short bass notes and occasional sustained sub anchors.
- Add 1/16 or 1/32 drum fills before section changes.
- Keep sub mono; let reese/noise layers provide width.

Avoid:
- Half-time dubstep snare placement unless requested.
- Long release tails on sub that smear the fast tempo.

## Breakbeat

Core feel:
- 125 to 145 BPM, syncopated drum breaks.
- Groove and edits matter more than grid-perfect density.
- Bass often supports breaks with stabs or rolling low phrases.

Useful moves:
- Warp breaks in `beats` or `complex` depending material.
- Add slice-like edits with clip split/copy.
- Humanize hat/percussion velocities.
- Use short delay throws and filtered transitions.

Avoid:
- Straightening all break timing.
- Over-layering impacts until the break loses identity.
