# Artist And Genre Research Notes

Use these notes to translate references into original, non-imitative Ableton
workflows. The goal is to borrow production principles: sound families, workflow
habits, arrangement tension, and mix priorities.

## Cross-Artist Synthesis

- Experimental bass and glitch hop depend on sound-design passes: record long
  explorations, automate or perform parameters, then keep the best fragments.
- Bass design is usually split into stable sub, animated mid layer, transient or
  noise detail, and space that can be muted or thrown into delays.
- Rhythm is not only note placement. The important moves are negative space,
  ghost hits, swing, short stutters, chopped breaks, and contrast between a
  clean anchor and noisy response.
- Tech house differs by subtraction: fewer elements, heavier processing per
  element, and a kick/bass relationship that drives the record.
- "Inspired by" should become a palette: filtered vowels, FM zaps, rubbery
  squelches, broken drums, precise low end, stereo micro-details, and careful
  rests.

## Tipper Palette

Research signal:
- Breakbeat, glitch hop, trip hop, ambient bass, and deep sound design are
  recurring descriptors. Optimal Breaks frames his catalog around breakbeat
  science, downtempo psychedelia, detailed programming, unusual stereo movement,
  and rhythm treated sculpturally: https://www.optimalbreaks.com/en/artists/tipper
- Lucidity Festival describes deep bass frequencies, intricate composition,
  "audio architecture", influential sound design, surround sound work, and live
  scratching/turntablism: https://lucidityfestival.com/musicians/tipper/
- Dancing Astronaut describes `Lattice` as psychedelic, bass-driven,
  half-time, multi-dimensional, and outside-the-norm frequency work:
  https://dancingastronaut.com/2017/10/tipper-champions-glitch-hop-release-4-track-lattice-ep/

Ableton translation:
- Favor audio chopping, warp-mode experiments, small stereo moves, and spectral
  or granular effects over one static synth patch.
- Use breaks, foley ticks, sub punctuation, and micro-edits that leave space.
- Keep the sub precise and let movement live in mid/high layers.

## G Jones Palette

Research signal:
- EDM.com's interview highlights Ableton-centered production, Moog Sub37 bass
  riffs, long live parameter jams recorded into Ableton, then chopping the best
  parts into songs: https://edm.com/interviews/g-jones-interview/
- The same interview emphasizes experimentation, rough ideas before perfect
  engineering, and letting unusual noise become the seed.

Ableton translation:
- Treat `Operator`, `Wavetable`, `Meld`, and any external source as jam sources.
- Automate filter, pitch bend, FM amount, drive, and delay during long clips,
  then split or copy the best moments.
- Use trap/breakbeat drum anchors with high-density edits and stark black/white
  contrast between silence, bass, and glitch hits.

## Shlump Palette

Research signal:
- Magnetic Magazine's 2026 interview emphasizes authenticity, musical lineages,
  jungle/atmospheric DnB influence, and taking a personal spin rather than
  surface borrowing: https://magneticmag.com/2026/04/shlump-interview/
- EDM Identity describes Shlump's "Alien Bass" as classic dubstep fused with
  West Coast bass culture, 140 BPM half-time drums, reeses, FM effects, hollow
  synths, hip-hop swing, pitch modulation, and futuristic textures:
  https://edmidentity.com/2022/12/06/shlumps-alien-shit-pt-ii-review/
- Run The Trap describes heavy tension-building, alien bass, and psychedelic
  production: https://runthetrap.com/2022/03/15/premiere-shlump-unleashes-mind-bending-forever-single-alongside-new-bass-ep-via-wakaan/

Ableton translation:
- Use 140 BPM half-time frames, FM zaps, vowel Auto Filter, reese layers, and
  playful pitch modulation.
- Layer "alien" sound design with classic dubstep restraint: kick, snare, sub,
  one talking bass answer, one strange top layer.

## Detox Unit Palette

Research signal:
- Lost In Sound's interview notes Ableton Live for composition and mixing, plus
  external synths, old effects units, and software synths for sound design:
  https://lostinsound.org/detox-unit-exclusive-mix-interview/
- The Rust Music profile describes analog/modular synths, old effects units,
  hard DAW focus, technical tinkering, detailed dirty/deep sound, glitches in
  intricate patterns, thunderous but carefully placed bass, and the role of
  empty space: https://www.therustmusic.net/rust-blog/2017/11/6/detox-unit-profile
- Conscious Electronic describes `Deviate` as off-kilter bass tracks with
  changing tempos, glitchy beats, scratches, and signature sounds:
  https://consciouselectronic.com/2019/06/29/detox-unit-ep-deviate/

Ableton translation:
- Build mechanical or circuit-like textures from Roar, Redux, Shifter, Spectral
  Time, Spectral Resonator, and short recorded foley.
- Use sparse bass hits and abrupt pauses to make detailed transients audible.
- Make room for scratches, reverse one-shots, and irregular fills.

## Chris Lake Palette

Research signal:
- MusicRadar's 2023 interview highlights low-end and groove as starting points,
  Ableton Live project-to-project track reuse, kick/bass combinations, and a
  modular sketch process: https://www.musicradar.com/news/chris-lake-interview
- The same interview emphasizes laptop-speaker sketching to judge the overall
  idea before fine low-end decisions.
- The Producer School's Chris Lake-style breakdown describes minimalism,
  kick/bass weight, short plucky bass, velocity-to-filter motion, saturation,
  sidechain, Erosion-like upper harmonics, sparse hats, tonal snare body, and
  cutoff automation as a primary arrangement tool:
  https://theproducerschool.com/blogs/featured-blogs/master-chris-lakes-tech-house-sound-complete-production-breakdown
- MusicRadar's 2025 interview reinforces quick flow, presets plus tweaks, and
  avoiding over-perfecting at the cost of musical quality:
  https://www.musicradar.com/artists/people-have-been-imitating-my-sound-for-a-long-time-but-now-someone-can-type-a-prompt-and-make-a-song-that-sounds-like-chris-lake-thats-wild-chris-lake-on-how-ai-is-putting-music-making-under-threat

Ableton translation:
- For tech house, start with kick, short bass, percussion, and cutoff movement.
- Use fewer layers, but inspect/tune each layer harder: transient, low-mid
  warmth, sidechain, and mobile-speaker harmonics.
- Use filter automation and small vocal/offbeat details rather than cinematic
  clutter.

## Practical Research-To-CLI Rules

- If the reference says "alien", use FM, comb, vowel, Spectral Resonator,
  pitch envelopes, and moving stereo delays.
- If it says "Tipper/Detox detail", use break slicing, foley clicks, spectral
  effects, short reverb rooms, and negative space.
- If it says "G Jones", favor live parameter recording, hard edits, Operator FM,
  trap/breakbeat syncopation, and abrupt contrast.
- If it says "Chris Lake/tech house", favor four-on-the-floor kick, plucky bass,
  sparse hats, velocity accents, cutoff automation, and club-functional dryness.
- If it says "deep dub", use sub stability, long delays, restrained drums,
  filtered noise, and space.
